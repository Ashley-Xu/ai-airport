## 1 · Core idea (Deliverable 1)

**Headline:**

> # "By the time you hear the alarm, the urgent steps are already taken care of."

**Sub-headline:** T+0:00. The axe swings in the flight deck. Zero warning. Zero seconds.
Watch the same event, twice.

**The idea in one paragraph:**

The AI-native airport does not give the CEO more screens. It gives the airport — and the
aircraft coming to it — a nervous system. Smart cameras and sensors as eyes (cabin,
flight deck, terminal, apron, and the ATC data link). A digital twin and agents as the
brain (understanding live scenes, summarizing video into plain language in seconds,
rehearsing responses, fusing data across the ecosystem). Robots, smart locks, guarded
flight controls, vehicles and reconfigurable infrastructure as hands. And where the
playbook runs out — because a playbook is a report about the *last* crisis — the nervous
system judges the scene it is actually seeing: it perceives, summarizes, recommends; a
human confirms in seconds, or has granted standing authority in advance. Irreversible
acts are always logged, streamed, and countermandable. The CEO/decision makers stop
being the person who finds out last, and become the person who decides what matters —
and how much judgment the machine is allowed to exercise.

**Why this answers the CEO's exact words:**

"We have screens and data everywhere, but we still find out too late."

→ The screens are not the problem. **A dashboard is a report about the airport, written
forty minutes ago. A playbook is worse: a report about the last crisis, written after
it.** Both are lagging instruments — one for the present, one for the future. The
report has no hands; the playbook has no eyes. Data without hands can only warn; rules
without perception can only repeat the last disaster in a new shape. So the human stays
in the middle of the loop, and the human — and the rulebook — is the latency.

Physical AI moves the human from the middle of the loop to the top of it, and replaces
the rulebook with judgment that can see.

## 2 · The event — latest reported facts (anonymised)

What the story is grounded in (news reporting through the ongoing investigations;
anonymised per locked decision — no airline, no names, no imagery):

> Last month, on a Dubai–Tel Aviv flight, a co-pilot attacked the captain with the
> cockpit's own crash axe and drove the aircraft into a dive — **nearly 14,000 feet in
> under 30 seconds**. Passengers forced their way into the flight deck and subdued him.
> The aircraft was flown to the nearest suitable airport by **two off-duty pilots who
> happened to be sitting in the cabin**. 174 people landed safely.
>
> Two details decide everything about this concept:
>
> **1 · The door.** The reason the passengers could get in: the captain — stabbed,
> bleeding — spent his remaining strength unlocking the flight-deck door from the
> inside. Since 9/11 that door cannot be opened from outside. If he had lost
> consciousness ten seconds earlier, the door would have been a wall, and everyone
> aboard was doomed.
>
> **2 · The controls.** The reason the aircraft could be flown: two off-duty crew were
> aboard by chance. Had they not been, there was nobody left who could fly the
> aircraft, and everyone aboard was doomed.
>
> And one detail that happened *before* the flight: investigators later reported the
> attacker **had been barred from flying by his home country's regulator over
> extremism concerns**. The airline that rostered him that morning did not know.
>
> **174 people lived because of a dying man's arm and two strangers' licences.
> Luck is not a safety system.**

The three doomed branches — what physical AI closes:

| # | The branch | Reality | The AI-native answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The door.** Captain cannot reach the lock → nobody gets in | He reached it. Barely | **A door that sees:** recognized faces outside, crew state inside, aircraft state — the scene judged in seconds and confirmed by a live human. No rule lookup, no luck required |
| 2 | **The controls.** Nobody aboard can fly → aircraft dives | Off-duty pilots, by chance | Autopilot intent-guard arrests the uncommanded dive; the AI stabilizes, guards the envelope and guides — the off-duty crew or a ground pilot flies it, auto-land only where certified |
| 3 | **The signal.** A ban existed in one regulator's file, invisible to the roster | Caught after, not before | Ecosystem data fusion: crew-risk signals cross jurisdictions before the roster is built |

Branches 1–2 are the nervous system in flight. Branch 3 is honest context: the same
idea (fuse what already exists, act before the event) one level up, in the ecosystem —
and it pre-empts the CEO's question "could this have been prevented on the ground?"

**The honest boundary v3 — stated on the page and spoken:**

> The airport cannot stop the axe from swinging. That did not happen in your sky. What
> physical AI changes is everything after the swing: the four minutes in the air
> (detect, summarize, judge, guard the envelope, open the door, fly without luck), the
> eight minutes on the ground (a choreographed response instead of a phone tree) — and
> one thing before it: a barred-from-flying signal that existed and reached no one.
> And judgment never runs free: every irreversible act is logged, streamed, and
> countermandable — human authority is a dial the CEO sets, not a checkbox.

## 6 · Sensitivity & honesty notes

- Both grounding events are real and violent; 150 people died in 2015, the captain last
  month was seriously injured, and investigations are ongoing (a terrorism angle is
  being investigated). Handling: anonymised, clinical, response-orchestration framing;
  the honest boundary stated on page and in script; no event imagery, ever; the
  emotional weight carried by the *structural* insight ("luck is not a safety system" /
  "rules fail quietly in scenes nobody enumerated"), never by graphic detail.
- **Using Germanwings as evidence is deliberate and defensible.** It is an 11-year-old,
  closed investigation, universally cited in aviation-safety discourse (EASA's own task
  force report names it), and here it argues *for* flexible intelligence over rigid
  rules — a safety-positive framing, not exploitation. It is used once, structurally,
  not as spectacle.
- **Flight-deck cameras are contested.** Post-2015 rules restrict cockpit voice/image
  recordings to accident investigation; pilot unions oppose live monitoring; IATA
  published a cabin-surveillance position paper in Jan 2026. The concept presents
  inward-facing flight-deck sensing as governance to be earned (event-triggered
  summaries, no continuous recording, human authority on irreversible acts, crew
  co-design) — and says so plainly on the page. Outward-facing cockpit-door
  surveillance (CDSS) is already certified and flying on A320-family aircraft under
  FAA/EASA STC; the extension is inward.
- **The smart door reverses 25 years of post-9/11 doctrine** — that is the point, and
  the argument is the two failures above. Safeguards: scene judgment rather than remote
  unlock; live human confirm by default; standing authority only when the CEO grants
  it; instant crew countermand; full logging and streaming; low-confidence scenes
  escalate.
- **Judgment ≠ unbounded autonomy.** The v5 shift makes the machine more flexible, so
  the governance has to be more explicit, not less: authority levels, trust records,
  revocability, and the divergence report are what make "it judges the scene"
  acceptable to a CEO and a regulator.
- **Autopilot takeover has real-world grounding:** DARPA's ALIAS programme demonstrated
  a robotic co-pilot autonomously landing a Boeing 737-800 simulator in a pilot-
  incapacitation scenario (2016); flight-envelope protection is standard on modern
  airliners. Intent-guard (rejecting uncommanded dives) plus stabilised diversion is a
  stated extrapolation, not a claim about today's certified systems.
- **Ownership honesty:** the airport does not own the aircraft. Cabin/flight-deck
  sensing is an airline/OEM/ecosystem capability; the airport is the federation point
  where the aircraft's nervous system meets the ground's, and where the CEO governs the
  authority dial. The brief's vision framing covers this; the walkthrough pre-answers
  the question.
