# 14:07 — The Diversion
## An AI-native airport concept · Core idea (Deliverable 1)

**Headline:**

> # "By the time you hear about it, it's already handled."

**The idea, in one paragraph:**

The AI-native airport does not give the CEO more screens. It gives the airport a
nervous system: sensors as eyes, a digital twin plus agents as the brain, robots and
automated infrastructure as hands. The airport senses its physical environment
continuously, rehearses the next hours thousands of times, and acts on its own —
escalating to humans only the decisions that carry judgment, money, or care. The CEO
stops being the person who finds out last, and becomes the person who decides what
matters.

**Why this reframes the CEO's complaint:**

The CEO says "we find out too late" despite screens and data everywhere. That is
because today's systems *report* the past: a dashboard turns red 40 minutes after the
queue already formed. And most airport problems are *physical* — queues, spills, stand
conflicts, bag jams. Data without hands can only warn; it cannot resolve. Physical AI
closes the loop: sense → simulate → act → inform. The human is moved from the middle
of the loop (making phone calls) to the top of it (making value judgments).

**The story we tell:**

One Tuesday. A thunderstorm diverts two inbound flights to your airport: 214 transit
passengers, one stand conflict, one baggage line about to overload. We follow the same
event through two airports on a single clock —

- **Today:** the event is discovered at 14:23, coordinated through 17 phone calls,
  turns the KPI wall red at 15:58, and reaches the CEO at 16:21. The decision that was
  needed at 14:10 arrives two hours stale.
- **AI-native:** the weather signal enters the twin at 12:37. By 13:20 the airport has
  already re-sequenced stands, rerouted autonomous baggage tractors, pre-positioned
  cleaning robots, and loaded dynamic signage. When the aircraft land at 14:07, there
  is no event. At 14:12 the CEO receives one notification: *resolved — one decision
  needs your approval.*

**What the CEO is asked to imagine (and approve, in the demo):**

A single card — the CEO's new inbox. Not 30 dashboards; the ~3 decisions per day that
genuinely need a human: spend, passenger care, risk appetite. Everything else, the
airport handles and reports.
